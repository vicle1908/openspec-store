## Context

The workspace already separates index freshness, generated-artifact ownership, and live runtime state across different authorities. The problem is not missing data sources; it is that caches, fixtures, databases, evidence, and rollback copies are mixed into one cleanup surface without a reviewed retention contract.

Retained state today includes generated indexes consumed by approved refresh contracts, persistent DuckDB/SQLite stores used by runtime services, logs retained by operators, and historical verification reports that document archive behavior. These categories need distinct protection semantics instead of a single “maybe disposable” bucket.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**

- Separate cleanup decisions into protected, reviewable, and candidate classes based on ownership and service function.
- Reuse existing index-freshness, watcher, and generated-state ownership contracts instead of replacing them.
- Produce a reviewed retention inventory that cleanup workflows consume as an exclusion and gating layer.
- Make “candidate for future reclamation” an explicit reviewed state, not a side effect of age, naming, or path depth.

**Non-Goals:**

- No branch or worktree retirement; that belongs to the lifecycle change.
- No automatic deletion, pruning, relocation, or archival of files.
- No new index mutation, watcher refresh, or replacement of the canonical refresh path.
- No runtime service redesign or database migration.
- No credential handling, hashing, or secret-surface exposure.

## Decisions

### 1. Classify by ownership and service function

Retained paths are classified by:

- what owns them today
- whether the path supports current indexes, services, evidence, rollback, or human review

A path that is generated by one owner but reused as evidence by another adopts the more protective classification.

### 2. Build the retention inventory from already-reviewed sources

The reviewed workspace inventory and freshness infrastructure already know which repositories and index artifacts are owned. The retention design consumes those results and adds:

- class and policy state
- protected owner
- provenance revision or evidence identity
- justification
- candidate status only when ownership review explicitly clears it

The operational inventory is stored at `~/Developer/.workspace-retention/retention-inventory.json`; the OpenSpec artifacts remain the normative contract.

### 3. Make protected classes non-negotiable in cleanup

Protected classes SHALL block cleanup proposals until a separate reviewed retirement policy exists. The protected classes are exactly the six defined in the spec: active index state, runtime state or database (including logs with active owners), tracked permanent fixture, retained evidence or report, retained rollback or snapshot, and operator-owned preserved file. The remaining classes, generated index or cache and temporary or ephemeral test fixture, are candidate classes governed by the review gate in Decision 4.

### 4. Treat candidate reclamation as policy, not action

A `RECLAIMABLE` item means it has current provenance, no active owner, documented regeneration or recovery behavior, and an explicit review record. It does not mean ready for deletion. Actual retirement remains subject to the lifecycle change and operator approval.

### 5. Avoid duplicating state across spec and runtime repositories

The retention contract should live close to workspace policy or runtime state, not deep inside one product repository. This keeps the policy visible to cross-repo cleanup tools and avoids policy drift across repo boundaries.

### 6. Preserve a single truth for index ownership

Generated index state SHALL continue to be explained by approved refresh/watcher ownership contracts. The retention policy records membership and protection; it does not rewrite the ownership rule.

### 7. Require provenance freshness

Every inventory entry SHALL bind to repository revision, evidence identity, or an explicit runtime observation time. Stale or missing provenance downgrades the entry to `REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

### 8. Name the inventory producer and implementation target

The retention inventory producer is a read-only validation and load tool colocated with the existing workspace index tooling. The implementation target is `openspec-store/scripts/workspace-retention/`, mirroring the established `openspec-store/scripts/knowledge-refresh/` pattern. The producer tool:

- Defines the canonical machine-readable inventory schema.
- Validates an inventory against the schema, rejecting a missing policy identity, stale or missing provenance, and any secret or request/response-body field.
- Loads the operational inventory from `~/Developer/.workspace-retention/retention-inventory.json`, preserving policy identity and rejecting an unapproved or malformed inventory.
- Applies provenance freshness checks, downgrading stale or missing provenance to `REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

The producer validates and loads; it SHALL NOT delete, relocate, or mutate any retained path. The first inventory's content is produced by this change's reviewed evidence capture (task 1.2), not by filesystem heuristics.

### 9. Machine-readable handoff schema

The handoff to `workspace-lifecycle-gated-cleanup` is the operational inventory document at `~/Developer/.workspace-retention/retention-inventory.json`. Its machine-readable shape is:

```json
{
  "policy": "workspace-artifact-retention-policy",
  "policy_version": "<semver>",
  "policy_identity": "<stable identity, e.g. approved-content digest>",
  "approval": {
    "approved": false,
    "digest": "<sha256 of approved entries>",
    "approved_at": "<ISO8601>",
    "approved_by": "<reviewer>"
  },
  "generated_at": "<ISO8601>",
  "entries": [
    {
      "path": "<canonical absolute path>",
      "class": "<one of the eight retention classes>",
      "policy_state": "<PROTECTED | REVIEW_REQUIRED | RECLAIMABLE | RECLAIMED>",
      "owner": "<owner identity or UNKNOWN>",
      "provenance": {
        "type": "<repo_revision | evidence_identity | runtime_observation>",
        "revision": "<git revision or null>",
        "evidence": "<evidence reference or null>",
        "observed_at": "<ISO8601 or null>"
      },
      "justification": "<reason for classification>",
      "last_reference": "<ISO8601 of last observed reference>",
      "candidate": false,
      "review": {
        "reviewed_at": "<ISO8601>",
        "reviewed_by": "<reviewer>",
        "note": "<optional>"
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

The lifecycle consumer reads `entries`, treats `PROTECTED` classes and states as exclusion lists, and treats `RECLAIMABLE` candidate artifacts as review-only proposals. The policy-state vocabulary (`PROTECTED`, `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, `RECLAIMABLE`, `RECLAIMED`) and the eight retention class names are shared verbatim between this change and `workspace-lifecycle-gated-cleanup`.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- Conservative classification slows cleanup velocity, but the workspace has already shown cost from accidental state removal.
- Overlapping ownership (index owner vs evidence consumer) can force more review; the design treats that ambiguity as protection rather than risk.
- Runtime ownership coverage will be incomplete early; the rule that uncertain ownership blocks reclamation is intentionally strict.
- Splitting lifecycle retirement from retention policy means two coordinated changes, but it avoids conflating workspace retirement with artifact classification.

## Resolved Decisions

### A. Policy and inventory storage

The retention policy and reviewed inventory SHALL live under the cross-repository workspace state area, with a versioned reference from the OpenSpec change. The OpenSpec artifacts define the contract; the runtime inventory is the current operational record consumed by cleanup.

### B. Mandatory versus optional owners

The first policy SHALL cover approved index/watchers, active processes, Orca workspaces, persistent databases/state stores, retained evidence, rollback snapshots, and operator-preserved files. LaunchAgents and container ownership MAY be reported by optional adapters, but an unavailable adapter SHALL produce `UNKNOWN` and SHALL block reclamation.

### C. Lifecycle dependency

`workspace-lifecycle-gated-cleanup` SHALL consume this retention inventory before classifying or proposing any artifact-related retirement. This change does not own branch/worktree retirement or deletion.
